# NUPP — Nearly Universal Principles of Projects (condensed)

Source: OMIMO NUPP manual (CC BY 4.0). Use these six principles as lenses when shaping a development roadmap — not as sections to fill in, but as checks on every phase and task you write.

## NUP1 — Prefer results and authenticity over affiliations
Don't let the roadmap be shaped by "this is how framework X says to do it." Borrow freely from Agile, waterfall, Kanban, whatever fits — judge every practice by whether it produces results for *this* project, not by loyalty to a methodology label.

## NUP2 — Preserve and optimize energy and resources
Apply the 80/20 rule: identify the 20% of steps that deliver 80% of the outcome and sequence the roadmap so those come first. Avoid microplanning that burns decision-making energy on low-value detail. Keep the roadmap simple enough that following it doesn't itself become a burden.

## NUP3 — Always be proactive
Plan before executing, and re-plan continuously as reality diverges from the plan — don't wait for problems to force a replan. Call out risks and unknowns explicitly in the roadmap rather than discovering them mid-task. Define roles/ownership per phase early rather than leaving it ambiguous.

## NUP4 — A chain is only as strong as its weakest link
Take a holistic view: don't optimize one domain (e.g. speed) while ignoring others (quality, scope, UX, data integrity, maintainability). For each phase, briefly check which domains are in play (scope, time, quality, UX, technical risk, communication) and make sure none is silently neglected.

## NUP5 — Never do anything without a clear purpose
Every phase, milestone, and task in the roadmap must trace back to the project's actual goal — not to "best practice" or "what's usually done." Before including a step, ask: what breaks in a parallel world where this step doesn't happen? If the answer is "nothing," cut it. Keep documents and artifacts as simple as their purpose requires — no template-filling for its own sake.

## NUP6 — Use repeatable elements
Where the same kind of work recurs (e.g. component reviews, data validation passes, QA checks), define it once as a reusable checklist or mini-workflow and reference it everywhere it applies, rather than re-describing it each time. Prefer short, repeatable cycles (daily/weekly-style checkpoints) over long undifferentiated stretches of work — repeatable structure reduces the cognitive cost of staying on track.
